The Osaka Series

An eight-part video series, Ideas in Motion: The Art & Science of Research Communication, developed for and delivered to the University of Osaka.

The series was made for graduate researchers facing a problem that has little to do with their science: how to make careful, specialized work understood by people outside their field — reviewers, policymakers, journalists, industry, and colleagues in other disciplines — and, for researchers in Japan, how to carry that work across to an international audience without losing what makes it distinctive.

It runs from first principles to practice. The outline gives the shape of it:

Unit 1
Understanding Your Audience
Who are you actually talking to — and why nearly every other choice follows from that.
Unit 2
Storytelling
How a well-told narrative carries a finding further than data alone.
Unit 3
Writing for Impact
Reaching academic and public audiences, the “so what” that makes work land, and posters people actually read.
Unit 4
Oral Presentations I — Structure & Delivery
Building a talk that holds together, and the quiet mechanics of delivery and Q&A.
Unit 5
Oral Presentations II — Data, Visuals & the Pitch
Working a conference, the elevator pitch, and picturing data so it's understood at a glance.
Unit 6
Media & Platforms
Stakeholders, press, and building a genuine presence online.
Unit 7
Criticism & Cross-Cultural Communication
Handling pushback and cultural difference — including the particular challenges facing social science in Japan.
Unit 8
Performance & Impact
Interdisciplinary and international collaboration, and where research communication is heading.

An earlier six-part series on Interdisciplinary Studies (2023) covered related ground — how ideas move between fields that don't share a vocabulary.
