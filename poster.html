The Poster That Lets You Walk Away

I had a chance to attend an economics poster event in Tokyo this year. A long room, rows of boards, and beside each one a researcher standing there, smiling, hoping someone would stop. Mostly, people didn't stop, they just moved slowly past. Sometimes a person leaned in to make out the small print, and often, as the presenter engaged them, I could feel their discomfort as they realized it wasn't something they were really interested in. They felt trapped in a conversation they couldn't easily leave.

The researcher did nothing wrong, except build a poster that looks like a paper — dense columns, small type, everything included so that nothing is left out. And that is why it either gets passed by — or becomes like fly paper that uninterested viewers are trying to get unstuck from.

Here's the part that surprises the people I work with: a poster does its job when it LETS a stranger walk away.

Think about who's actually in that corridor. Even in your own field, a passerby has maybe three seconds and a dozen other boards to reach. They are not going to read your methods. What they can do, from two meters off, is take in one clear sentence — the single real-life thing you found — and then decide whether they want more. If yes, they step closer already interested, and a conversation happens. If no, they move on without guilt, and you're free for the next person. It's the audience thing again: and here your headline tries to frame your work for your largest audience.

One simple headline finding, big enough to read across the aisle. Some supporting material off to the side for the ones who lean in. A QR code for those who want to dig deeper into your work. The researcher always knows which one sentence matters. They've just never been given permission to let the rest go!

And with this approach, the focus becomes about person-to-person communication, new connections, building confidence, and a richer conference experience.

And not catching flies.
