Word Up!

I came across an article on Hegel this morning, whom I never understood. I hated philosophy in college. Hegel? I’d prefer a bagel. I couldn’t get Kant. I was locked out of Locke, and when they gave me Schoppenhauer, I’d go shop for an hour. Plato? He was Greek to me.

But today I learned Hegel said, approximately, an idea only becomes real when it passes between minds — when it’s recognized, taken up by someone else. When a person can hand it to another person.

“AI was a real help,” a student told me recently. “It organized my data, it caught patterns I’d missed, it even drafted a first version of my analysis. But when I try to summarize it, I go blank. It can’t do that part for me.”

I hear versions of this more and more, and I’m empathetic. After all, I use these tools every day; so do many people I work with. They’re astonishing. They make our work go faster, be sharper, often come out better than we could manage alone.

And yet my student had put her finger on something real. She still lives in the world with me and other people. She still needs to find the right words to let others understand her work and why it matters. Because, for all the powers of AI, it can’t be a human across the table from you or in an audience and decide your idea is worth carrying into their own work. Meaning isn’t in the text. It’s in the uptake, and the uptake is human.

As the generating gets automated, human connection is still the place where the meaning is actually made. That hasn’t changed, and I don’t think it will.
