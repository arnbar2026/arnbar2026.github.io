Interdisciplinary Studies Series

A six-part introductory video series, An Introduction to Interdisciplinary Studies, developed for and delivered to a major Japanese university (2023).

The series introduces interdisciplinary studies — IDS — to graduate and undergraduate researchers: what the interdisciplinary mindset actually is, how it works in practice, and why a faster, more interconnected world increasingly depends on people who can think across the boundaries of a single field.

It runs from first principles to real-world practice, using Japanese cases — aging, education, agriculture, the climate — as its proving ground. The outline gives the shape of it:

Part 1
Interdisciplinary Studies in a Complex World
Why a faster, more interconnected world has outgrown single fields — and how interdisciplinary work became the natural path to real-world solutions.
Part 2
The Merits & Limits of Disciplinarity
How the disciplines took shape, why each sees only its own slice of reality, and what the Human Genome Project shows about crossing the silos.
Part 3
The Interdisciplinary Mindset
The habits of mind collaboration runs on — critical thinking, wide curiosity, perspective-taking, ethics, and the nerve to speak up when experts miss the obvious.
Part 4
The Procedures of Interdisciplinary Research
A ten-step method — from framing the question to integrating and testing insights — put to work on the puzzle of reforming Japanese education.
Part 5
New Tools for Analysis & Integration
The digital tools that now speed research, analysis, and collaboration — and the judgment, intuition, and reflection they still can't replace.
Part 6
Current Challenges & Opportunities
Where the work matters most now — the climate, aging, agriculture — and the interdisciplinary careers opening for those ready to turn “you” into “we.”

The later eight-part Research Communication Series (2025) built on this ground — turning from how ideas move between fields to how researchers carry their work across to the wider world.
